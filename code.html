History

The place where languages create connection.

In schools and in culture, Langua emerged as a way to bring close a powerful idea: that people should learn to listen to one another, not only through words, but through rhythm, accent, memory, and place.

At its core, it is an old tradition: when a community understands that a language is not a closed border, but a path toward human continuity, it becomes an object of collective affection, education, and identity.

  • 1910s

    The school of culture

    Language begins to be seen as a way to understand history itself, not just the speech of one country.

  • 1950s

    Culture of dialogue

    A new idea of international connection makes languages feel like a living network of human exchange.

  • Today

    A festival of reading

    Langua becomes a way to illuminate the many worlds living in every word, accent, and cultural encounter.
